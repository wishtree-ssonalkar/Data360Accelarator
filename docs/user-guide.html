Getting started

  1. Open the appUse the link from your administrator (for a local demo: http://localhost:8501).
  2. Pick the business unitIn the left sidebar, choose the business under Business unit. Every page then shows only that business's data.
  3. Check how fresh the data isThe line under each page title shows "data as of" a date. The Data pipeline page shows when the last refresh ran.
  4. NavigateThe menu is grouped: Overview, Customers, Analytics, AI, Platform and (for administrators) Admin.
Tips that work on every page Hover over a chart to see exact values · click legend items to hide or show a series · use the icons above a table to search, sort, expand to full screen or download it as CSV.

Customer 360

Answers: Everything we know about one customer, from every system.

  1. SearchType a name, email, phone or customer id. Partial text works ("Riya", "98765", "@gmail").
  2. Pick the personChoose from the list (sorted by lifetime spend).
  3. Read the headerTier, RFM segment, lifecycle stage, churn risk, marketing consent, the systems this person appears in, and a Next best action.
  4. Check the numbersLifetime revenue, orders (and refunds), average order value, days since last order, RFM score, support tickets, recent web sessions and abandoned carts.
  5. Explore the tabsTimeline (everything, newest first) · Orders (items and spend by category) · Support · Web & app · Source records (the original records merged into this profile, so you can see why they were matched).
Use it before a callSupport and sales teams can see a customer's recent orders, refunds and open complaints in one place before calling back.

Segments & RFM

Answers: What kinds of customers do we have, and who should a campaign target?

  • Segment map: each box is a segment; size = number of customers. Hover for revenue.
  • RFM grid: customers by recency (left to right, 5 = most recent) and frequency (bottom to top). Top-right = best customers; top-left = valuable customers who have gone quiet.
  • Segment table: customers, lifetime revenue, revenue share, average days since order, average orders, and how many have marketing consent.

Build and export an audience

  1. Choose segmentse.g. "At Risk" and "Cannot Lose Them".
  2. Narrow it downBy tier and city. Keep Only marketing opt-ins on for promotional messages.
  3. Check the size and valueAudience size, their lifetime revenue and their most common favourite category.
  4. ExportClick Export audience (CSV) and load the file into your email, SMS, WhatsApp or ads tool.

Retention & churn

Answers: Do customers come back, and who is about to leave?

  • Headline numbers: repeat rate, median days between orders, share who came back within 3 months, high-risk customers and their value, churned share.
  • Cohort grid: each row is the month customers first bought; each column is months later; the cell is the % who bought again that month. Darker = better. Compare rows to see whether retention is improving.
  • Churn risk by tier and days since last order histogram.
  • Win-back list: valuable customers at high risk, with their usual gap between orders. Hand it to the retention team.

Conversion funnel

Answers: Where do website and app visitors drop off?

  • Choose the period with the slider and the devices to include.
  • Read the funnel: visited → viewed a product → added to cart → started checkout → purchased.
  • Key rates: conversion, cart abandonment, checkout completion, and the share of visits linked to a known customer.
  • The trend by device shows whether, for example, the mobile site converts worse than the app.

Product performance

Answers: What sells, what earns, and what causes problems?

  • Filter by category.
  • Revenue and margin by category; top 12 products by revenue.
  • Problem products chart: refund rate against number of support tickets, with bubble size = revenue. Products in the top-right are returned often and complained about: check sizing, photos, packaging or the supplier.
  • Full product table with units, buyers, margin %, refund rate, tickets, CSAT, views and add-to-carts.

Ask your data (AI)

Ask a question in plain English; the assistant writes the database query, runs it on this business's data and shows a chart, a short summary and the data.

Good questions to try

  • Who are my top 20 customers by lifetime revenue?
  • Which Gold or Platinum customers in Pune haven't ordered in 90 days?
  • Show monthly revenue for the last 12 months by channel.
  • Which cities have the most At Risk customers, and how much are they worth?
  • What is the average order value by channel this year?
  • Which products have the highest refund rate and the most complaints?
  • How many new customers did we get each month this year?
  • Compare the 3-month repeat rate of customers acquired in January versus June.

Tips

  • Be specific about time ("last 6 months"), place ("in Mumbai") and measure ("by revenue").
  • Ask follow-ups: "now only for the app channel", "show it as a table".
  • Open How this was answered to see the SQL query. Analysts can check or reuse it.
  • The example question buttons work even when no AI provider is configured. The sidebar shows which provider and model are answering.
  • Use Clear conversation in the sidebar to start a new topic.
Check important numbersThe assistant is usually right, but like any AI it can misread a question. For decisions, check the query under "How this was answered" or confirm on the matching dashboard.

Data pipeline

Mostly for administrators, but useful to everyone to check that data is fresh and complete:

  • Last pipeline run and status; how many sources are connected and failing.
  • Tabs: Architecture & lineage · Sources (last load and errors) · Run history · Identity resolution (how many duplicates were merged) · Data quality · Business rules.
  • Administrators can start a refresh with Run now; the page updates when it finishes.

Weekly routines

Leadership · Monday

Executive overview: revenue trend, active customers, value at risk. Ask the AI one question about anything unusual.

Retention · Weekly

Retention page win-back list + "Who needs attention". Export the At Risk / Cannot Lose Them audience.

Marketing · Per campaign

Segments → build audience → export CSV. Compare segment sizes after the campaign.

CX / Support · Daily

Customer 360 before callbacks; open tickets on high-value customers.

Merchandising · Monthly

Problem products chart; margin by category; refund trends.

Digital · Weekly

Funnel by device; abandoned carts; conversion trend.
