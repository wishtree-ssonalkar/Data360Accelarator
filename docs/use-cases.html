Target clients

WishUnify's standard model is built around customers who buy things (orders, products), with support and website behaviour on top. Fit by industry:

IndustryFitWhy
Omnichannel retail (fashion, footwear, electronics, home, beauty)ExcellentOnline + store + CRM + support is exactly the problem WishUnify solves; POS phone matching links walk-in buyers.
D2C and e-commerce brandsExcellentShopify/WooCommerce, marketplaces, helpdesk, web analytics; repeat purchase and retention matter most.
Restaurants, QSR, cafés, cloud kitchensVery goodPOS + own app + delivery platform orders; frequency and lapsing guests drive revenue.
Pharmacy, grocery, convenience chainsVery goodHigh-frequency purchases, loyalty phone numbers at billing, refill / repeat patterns.
Consumer services (salons, spas, fitness, clinics, auto service)GoodBookings/invoices map to orders; services map to products; visit frequency and churn.
Hospitality and travelGood with extensionBookings map to orders; stays, nights and loyalty points may need extra fields.
Education / EdTechGood with extensionEnrolments as orders, courses as products; engagement events.
Subscriptions / SaaS / telecomNeeds extensionNeeds a subscription entity (plan, MRR, renewals) added to the model.
Banking, insurance, lendingNeeds extensionAccounts, policies and claims are different entities; plus stricter compliance.
B2B distributors / manufacturersPartialWorks for orders by customer account; contact-level identity and B2B hierarchies need extension.

Buying signals

  • "We don't know how many unique customers we have."
  • "Online and store customers are completely separate for us."
  • "Campaign lists are pulled manually into Excel every time."
  • "We don't know who stopped buying until it's too late."
  • "Our CDP quote / renewal is too expensive." or "We bought a CDP and nobody uses it."
  • "Support doesn't know a caller is one of our best customers."
  • Launching a loyalty programme, an app, or moving to omnichannel.
  • Recently migrated data platform (e.g. a WishBridge migration): natural next step is Customer 360 on the new platform.

Good fit vs not yet a fit

Good fit

Transactions with a customer identifier (email, phone or id) in at least one system; 12+ months of history; a business owner who wants to act on segments.

Not yet a fit

No customer identifiers at all (anonymous cash sales only); needs real-time personalisation in milliseconds; needs to push audiences into ad platforms automatically today; very large data (billions of rows) without moving to a cloud warehouse.

Worked examples

Illustrative scenariosThese show the kind of setup, findings and actions to expect. Examples 1 and 2 use the same sources and rules as the two demo businesses; the findings are typical outcomes, not measured results from a real client.

1. Omnichannel footwear retailer

Client: 60 stores + website + app, ~200k customers. (This is the "Stride Footwear" sample in the demo.)

Sources: Shopify customers and orders · store POS export (phone only) · Salesforce contacts · Zendesk tickets · web/app events · product catalog.

What WishUnify finds:

  • Store receipts are linked to online customers by phone, revealing that a large share of top online customers also buy in store.
  • Duplicate Shopify accounts and CRM contacts collapse into one profile per person.
  • "At Risk" and "Cannot Lose Them" segments with high past spend, plus a value at risk figure for leadership.
  • A few running-shoe models with high refund rates and "size / fit" tickets.

Actions: win-back campaign to opted-in At Risk Gold customers; size-guide fix for the problem models; VIP early access for Champions.

2. D2C home-decor brand

Client: online-first, marketplace sales, one experience store, ~80k customers. (The "UrbanLeaf Home" sample.)

Sources: Shopify · marketplace order reports (CSV upload) · Freshdesk tickets · GA4 events export.

Business rules: longer purchase cycle, so "active" = bought within 365 days and higher tier thresholds.

What WishUnify finds: repeat purchases cluster around 3–4 months after a furniture order (accessories); "damaged in transit" tickets concentrate on two product lines; mobile web converts much worse than desktop.

Actions: automated accessory recommendations at 90 days; packaging change with the courier; mobile checkout fix.

3. Restaurant chain (QSR)

Client: 40 outlets, own ordering app, delivery partners, loyalty by phone number.

Sources: POS database (SQL Server, incremental by bill time) · app orders (PostgreSQL) · delivery-partner settlement reports (CSV) · feedback forms (CSV).

Mapping notes: bills → orders (channel "Dine-in", "Takeaway", "App", "Delivery"); menu items → products; feedback → tickets with CSAT.

What WishUnify finds: guests who switched from dine-in to delivery-only; weekly regulars who lapsed after a bad rating; best-selling combos by outlet.

Actions: "we miss you" offers to lapsed regulars; service recovery calls after low CSAT; outlet-level menu decisions.

4. Pharmacy chain

Client: 150 stores + e-pharmacy app, loyalty by mobile number.

Sources: store billing (Oracle, via ODBC) · app orders (REST API) · product master (Excel upload) · call-centre tickets.

What WishUnify finds: chronic-care customers whose regular refill gap is ~30 days, flagged as churn risk when they pass 2× their usual gap; app-only vs store-only vs both customers and their value.

Actions: refill reminders before the usual gap; move high-value store-only customers to the app with home delivery.

PrivacyLoad purchase data, not prescriptions or diagnoses; check local health-data rules and keep the AI summary off if required.

15-minute demo script

  1. The problem (1 min)"Your customer data lives in 5 systems. Here is what it looks like when it's unified."
  2. Executive overview (2 min)Unified profiles from N source records; revenue by channel; value at risk; "Who needs attention this week".
  3. Customer 360 (3 min)Open a top customer: tier, segment, churn risk, next best action; Timeline across store, web and support; Source records tab shows how they were matched.
  4. Segments (3 min)Segment map and RFM grid; build an "At Risk + Cannot Lose Them, opted-in, Mumbai" audience; export CSV.
  5. Retention and products (2 min)Cohort grid; problem-products chart.
  6. Ask your data (2 min)"Which cities have the most At Risk customers and what are they worth?" Show the chart, the summary and the SQL.
  7. Admin (2 min)Sources & connections: Test connection shows raw and mapped rows; Mappings: dropdown column picker; Run now.

Positioning and pitch

One-linerWishUnify gives you a single view of every customer across your store, online, CRM and support systems, with segments, churn risk and plain-English answers, in weeks, on your own infrastructure, without a CDP licence.
  • Versus commercial CDPs: no licence, data stays with the client, customizable mappings and rules, faster to tailor.
  • Versus building in-house: weeks instead of months; proven model, connectors, identity matching, dashboards and AI already built.
  • Versus BI dashboards alone: BI shows totals; WishUnify first creates the unified customer that BI tools can't, then adds segments and actions.
  • With WishBridge: migrate the data platform with WishBridge, then unify customers on it with WishUnify.
